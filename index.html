Board format & coordinates

Coordinates are 1-based, with (1, 1) at the bottom left. Use colors R, G, B, Y and directions u, d, l, r. The grid is rectangular; list blocked cells in walls. The optional wallColor (R or G; blue when omitted) only changes how the walls look. Optional mechanic arrays may be omitted.

{
  "version": 1, "id": "my-board", "title": "My board",
  "width": 5, "height": 4,
  "balls": [{"color":"R","x":1,"y":1}],
  "goals": [{"color":"R","x":5,"y":4}],
  "walls": [{"x":3,"y":2}],
  "arrows": [{"x":3,"y":1,"direction":"u","rotating":true,"type":"flip"}],
  "solution": "Rr(->r)u"
}

gates: {id, x, y, active}. switches: {id, x, y, controls: [gate or teleport IDs], on?}; optional on: true starts the switch green. teleports: one entry per endpoint, {id, x, y, color, active}; color is R/G/B/Y or "*" (universal). Partners follow from the colors: same-color endpoints pair up, two universals pair with each other, and a lone universal pairs with the single endpoint of every other color. hexes: {id, x, y, value}. Negative hex values subtract from the score.

Puzzles

How to play

Select one ball and choose a direction. It keeps moving until it hits a wall, a boundary, or another ball. Dock every ball on a matching goal.
◎

Stop to dock

A matching goal docks a ball only when it stops there. Docked balls stay put and block other balls.

↱

Follow the arrows

Arrows redirect a move as you enter. Red arrows keep turning, but freeze while a ball moves: wait for the direction you want before you move.

◉

Stop on a switch

Stop on a switch to toggle it red/green and change its linked walls or teleports. A wall group stays unchanged if turning it on would trap a ball inside a wall.

Use a teleport

Colored teleports accept matching balls; gray universal teleports accept any color. Both preserve direction. If the exit is occupied or switched off, the ball stops at the entrance.

Use a tunnel

Two small triangles mark a gap in the outline. Tunnels come in linked pairs: a ball that moves through one gap comes out of the partner gap, moving away from it. If the exit is occupied, the ball stops at the entrance.

⬡

One-use hexagons

Green subtracts; red adds. Passing through consumes a hexagon for every ball. One move always adds one to the move score.

↶

History navigation

Ctrl+Z deletes the last move, only at the end of the route. Navigate without deleting: Shift+Z or Home = Start; Z or PgUp = Previous; X or PgDn = Next; Shift+X or End = End.

Solution notation

Rur,G3ld,Bu means red up/right, green 3 left/down, blue up. Numbering is fixed from the starting board, left to right and bottom to top. Omit the number when only one movable ball of that color remains. Ru(->l)d chooses left at the first rotating arrow during the upward move, then moves down. If that arrow flips between two opposite directions and a later arrow turns through all four, add that later arrow's direction: Ru(->lr).

Solver

Advanced

Max score only looks for routes scoring at most that value. Certify sets it from the current route.
Auto picks bidirectional search (forward and reverse alternately) when the board allows it (at least 3 balls, no one-time hexagons), otherwise forward. Forward is plain breadth-first search from the start: it works on every board but stores the most states. Both prove optimality when they finish. Beam is faster than BFS and certifies optimality only on boards without hexagons when no state ever exceeded the beam width; otherwise it gives an upper bound.
